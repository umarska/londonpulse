How the outlook is made

Past counts. Future calendar.
Clear boundaries.

A reproducible forecasting experiment, with every prediction tied to a date.

01

Keep the city's record.

Official TfL daily cycle hire totals form the historical record. The project checks dates, duplicates, gaps and counts before modelling. Source files and checksums are recorded so the evidence can be inspected.

02

Freeze the information.

A seven-day forecast is issued at a known origin. Recent demand and trailing statistics stop at that date. Future dates and their calendar features are available; future hire counts are not.

03

Earn the extra complexity.

Simple forecasting baselines set the standard. Candidate models are compared using earlier validation weeks, then the selected approach is evaluated on later, held-out weeks.

04

Show the room for error.

Prediction ranges are calibrated on Sunday forecast origins. Their coverage and width are measured on later Sunday-origin weeks. Coverage is unassessed for other origin weekdays, including the latest saved outlook.

What the model knows

  • Daily hire counts available by the origin
  • Patterns in recent and seasonal demand
  • Known calendar information for future dates

What is beyond this model

  • Unknown future weather and service disruption
  • Unrecorded events or changes in travel behaviour
  • Station availability, destinations or individual journeys

A forecast is a planning input.

City-wide totals cannot prescribe bicycle redistribution. This research app does not represent a live TfL service: the latest outlook starts from the saved source cutoff. Historical counts can be revised after publication, so this retrospective test is not a reconstruction of each day's original data feed. Two source zeros during the September 2022 scheme shutdown are preserved as service counts, not interpreted as zero underlying demand.
